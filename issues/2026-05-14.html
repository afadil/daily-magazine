01 · Productivity ● Notion ● May 13 For You

The doc becomes the agent's desk.

Notion 3.5 ships a developer platform that turns the workspace into an orchestration layer. Notion Workers are a hosted runtime — your coding agent writes the script, the CLI deploys it in a sandbox, and a database with an API on the other end (Salesforce, Zendesk, Postgres) becomes a synced Notion database. Workers are free during beta, then run on Notion credits starting August 11.

The bigger move is the External Agents API: users assign work to Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, and Decagon agents from inside Notion, with status streamed back as if they were native. Custom Agents launched in February already have a million instances built — this opens the doors to let third-party agents share a doc.

Data centers, but in low orbit.

The Wall Street Journal reports Google is in advanced talks with SpaceX to launch orbital data centers, the next leg of Project Suncatcher (announced late 2025, with prototype satellites due 2027). SpaceX is selling the pitch into its planned $1.75T IPO: solar-powered, vacuum-cooled compute, far from grid bottlenecks.

The math doesn't pencil today — terrestrial wins on per-FLOP cost once you add launch — but Musk's stated ambition is a million-satellite constellation, and Google is parallel-shopping with other rocket providers. Treat this as a long-horizon bet that AI training will outgrow what utilities can supply on Earth.

10 · Weird Science ● Quantum ● May 13

Instant W-states.

Researchers in Japan have demonstrated a way to detect quantum W-states instantly — a class of entangled multi-particle states that, unlike GHZ states, retain entanglement even when one particle is lost. Robustness has always been their pitch; measuring them quickly without destroying them has been the bottleneck.

The result puts W-state quantum networks one step closer to practical. If you have ever needed a metaphor for distributed systems that survive node failure — this is the physics version of that.

Cloudflare Engineering · May 13, 2026

The browser pool, moved to Containers.

Ruskin Constant, Rui Figueira, Sofia Cardita · blog.cloudflare.com

Browser Run is Cloudflare's headless-browser-as-a-service — the substrate behind end-to-end tests, scraping pipelines, PDF rendering, and the rapidly growing population of AI agents that want to click on real websites. The piece is a postmortem of the migration off shared infrastructure with Browser Isolation, and the systems-design choices that came with it. Three matter.

4×
Concurrent browsers per pool, up from 30 to 120
60/min
Cold-start spinup via Workers binding, up from 15/min
−50%
P95 latency on Quick Actions, by killing the websocket hop

One: pre-warmed regional pools instead of global Durable-Object placement. The previous topology let DO-backed containers spin up wherever the request landed; in practice the WebSocket between the DO and the headless Chrome was the dominant latency. The team carved the world into regions, kept hot browsers idling per region, and routes the request to the pool — not the other way around. This is the same shape as nginx upstream pools, ported to a planet-scale DO mesh.

Two: KV → D1 + Queues for browser assignment. Cloudflare KV's eventual consistency (up to a 30-second lag) was perfectly fine for config but disastrous for "which browser is currently allocated to which session" — two callers could race-claim the same instance. They moved the state to D1, where a single SQLite transaction makes the assignment atomic, then batched the writes 100 at a time through Queues. The result: throughput per region jumped from ~5,000 containers to 500,000, with P95 write latency at 0.1ms.

Three: direct HTTP, no WebSocket round-trip. Quick Actions (single-shot calls: render this PDF, snapshot this URL) used to traverse Worker → DO → WebSocket → browser. They collapsed it to Worker → Container HTTP, dropping the multi-step handshake for the common case. WebSocket is still there for long sessions where you actually need duplex.

We're committed to building on our own platform wherever feasible so that we can feel and fix any pain points ahead of external customers. — Cloudflare Browser Run team

The framing matters: this is Cloudflare deliberately running a high-churn, latency-sensitive workload on the same primitives — Workers, DOs, D1, Queues, Containers — they sell. The internal users are the regression test. The substantive takeaway for senior engineers: when KV's lag becomes a correctness bug instead of a UX one, that's the signal to move to a transactional store. And when your protocol's setup cost dominates your steady-state cost, the right move is to collapse the protocol, not optimize the hops.
