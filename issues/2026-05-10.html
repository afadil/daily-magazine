GPT-5.5-Cyber arrives — and it isn't a ChatGPT update. For You

On Friday OpenAI dropped GPT-5.5-Cyber, a model fine-tuned for security operations and shipped behind a "Trusted Access" tier rather than the consumer product. The announcement leans hard on triage: incident response, vulnerability research, and patch generation are the three workloads called out by name.

OpenAI is gating the model behind enterprise verification because it can meaningfully accelerate exploit development as well — the same dual-use line Anthropic walked when it shipped cyber-tuned variants of Claude. If you do appsec or run a red team, this is the one to evaluate this week.

Meta, Amazon, Microsoft and Alphabet have now collectively signalled roughly $725B of 2026 capex — up more than 75% year-on-year — with nearly the entire delta earmarked for data centres, custom silicon, GPUs, and AI training runs. Spread 07 · Astronomy · machine learning

An AI tool called RAVEN just sifted 100+ planets out of TESS data.

NASA confirmed this week that RAVEN — a transit-signal classifier trained on the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite archive — has surfaced more than one hundred exoplanets including 31 brand-new worlds. The interesting catches sit in the so-called Neptunian desert, where planets of a certain mass aren't supposed to exist, and on ultra-short orbits under twenty-four hours.

RAVEN didn't find anything new in the universe; it found things humans had already collected and never looked at. The bottleneck wasn't telescope time. It was attention.

Curiosity finds the most diverse organic chemistry yet seen on Mars.

7 new molecules Cumberland mudstone Billions of yrs old

A re-analysis of samples from the Cumberland drill site has turned up seven previously undetected organic molecules preserved in clay-rich rock that once held standing water. None of them are biosignatures on their own, but the diversity — long-chain hydrocarbons sitting alongside sulphur-bearing organics — is the most varied set the rover has produced.

The team used a one-shot pyrolysis technique they had been saving for high-confidence targets, which is why the result took a decade to surface from samples drilled in 2013. The chemistry is consistent with prebiotic process; the absence of extant biology, equally so.

Spread 09 · Quantum optics · Oxford · Nature

Quadsqueezing: the fourth-order squeeze that wasn't supposed to be measurable.

Oxford physicists have demonstrated quadsqueezing, a fourth-order quantum effect that compresses uncertainty along a higher-order axis of the optical phase space than the standard two-mode squeeze familiar from gravitational-wave detectors. In plain terms: where conventional squeezing trades amplitude noise for phase noise on a single observable, quadsqueezing re-allocates uncertainty across a quartic combination of quadratures — a regime theorists had described but no lab had actually produced. The team used a tailored nonlinear medium and homodyne tomography to verify the negative-Wigner-function signature, and reports a route to using the effect for sensing tasks that hit the standard quantum limit. The result widens the toolkit for any experiment whose precision is bottlenecked by shot noise — interferometric astronomy, dark-matter searches, and chip-scale magnetometry are the three application domains the paper calls out.

A rainforest katydid was caught switching from hot pink to forest green.

Researchers in Ecuador documented a Markia-species katydid sliding through a colour change that the field had assumed impossible at this taxonomic level — pink in low light, green in high. Reversible carotenoid-mediated colour change shows up in cephalopods and a few fish; a katydid making the same trick on a longer timescale forces a small rewrite of what insect cuticle chromatophores can do.

The paper notes the animal isn't camouflaging in the moment — it's tracking the dominant wavelength of its microhabitat over hours. Slow camouflage. A new category, basically.

Architecture in the Wild

Cloudflare ate its own dogfood and ended up with 3,683 internal AI users.

blog.cloudflare.com / Ayush Thakur, Scott Roe-Meschke, Rajesh Bhatia / April 20, 2026
Eleven months ago Cloudflare formed a tiger team it named iMARS — Internal MCP Agent/Server Rollout Squad — and pointed it at the same problem every large engineering org now has: how do you give thousands of developers safe, governed access to frontier models without the result looking like a spaghetti pile of personal API keys? The answer they describe in this April 20 piece is a vertical stack built almost entirely on shipping Cloudflare products: AI Gateway as the LLM proxy and cost meter, Workers AI for cheaper open-weight inference (Kimi K2.5 handles ~15% of their AI code review traffic), an MCP Portal exposing 13 production servers and 182+ tools, and a Backstage instance cataloguing 2,055 services as the grounding context every agent reads. The architectural insight is the proxy: by forcing every model call through a single Worker, they get a control plane — quotas, redaction, ZDR enforcement, BYOK — that direct SDK calls cannot. The numbers they share are the kind that make you re-evaluate your own rollout: 60% of the company actively using the tooling, 93% in R&D, weekly merge requests up from ~5,600 to a peak of 10,952. Worth reading if you are inside an org that is still hand-distributing OpenAI keys.

"Frontier models still do the hard agentic work, but everything routed through the gateway is a control surface we own — not a contract we hope our vendor honours." — iMARS team, paraphrased from the post
